How this is calculated

swing = margin in the "to" election − margin in the "from" election

  • Margin is the Democratic share minus the Republican share, using shares of all votes cast, not the two-party vote. A positive swing is toward Democrats; a negative swing is toward Republicans. We show both the same way.
  • Elections held. 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026. President in 2020 and 2024, U.S. Senate where a seat was up, U.S. House every two years. Every election is stored down to the county.
  • Different offices. You can compare a presidential result with a House or Senate result, and the page says when you do. The candidates differ, so some of the swing is about them, not the place.
  • Counties are the steady unit. County lines did not move between 2018 and 2026, except in Connecticut. Use counties to compare across the whole period.
  • District lines changed. Districts are compared only when both elections used the same lines. Pennsylvania drew new lines for 2022, so 2018 and 2020 districts cannot be compared with 2022, 2024 or 2026 districts.
  • Unopposed races have no two-sided margin. Their places are excluded and the count of exclusions is shown.
  • Boundaries changed; not comparable. A place whose FIPS code does not exist in both years is listed but not scored. Connecticut's planning regions are the main case.
  • Alaska reports by state house district, not county, and is shown that way.
  • Uncertified counts are labelled until the state certifies.

Method version 1.1 · Characteristics: Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020 to 2024 five-year estimates.
